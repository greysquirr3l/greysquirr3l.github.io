Troubleshooting

What to check first, by symptom. Every entry says what the SDK refuses, what it will not do for you, and what you change. None of them asks you to print a token or a secret, and none of them should: the SDK keeps credentials out of its errors and Debug output (see the error taxonomies), and a log line you add yourself should carry the correlation id and the status, never an Authorization header or a request body.

Each section below is the negative path of one or more recipes in the offline recipes, and the recipe shows the failure happening.

What an error carries

Section titled “What an error carries”

MussError and TeamsError answer four questions without parsing text: is_retryable(), retry_after(), status() and correlation_id(). Log those four. For a Graph call the error also carries the request id Graph reported and the client-request-id the SDK sent, so Microsoft support or your own logs can match the failure to a call. See Microsoft Graph and outbound messages.

401 versus 403

Section titled “401 versus 403”

The two statuses mean different things and are fixed in different places.

Status Meaning Usual cause What you change
401 The credential was not accepted. Inbound: a missing, malformed, expired or wrongly signed token, a token for another app id, or a request that did not come from Teams. Outbound: a wrong or expired client secret, a wrong tenant, or a token minted for another audience. The credential, the tenant id or the app id. Not a permission.
403 The credential was accepted and the operation is not permitted. Outbound Graph: an application permission was never granted or consented. Connector: the app is not in the conversation, or the person blocked the bot. In a card handler: your own authorization hook said no, or no hook is registered. The consent, the install, or your hook. Not the secret.

Inbound, a request that fails authentication is answered 401 or 403 before it reaches anything else (the table in the routing pipeline lists the other statuses: 400, 413 and 503). The simulation shows it:

prints a token signed by another key is refused with HTTP 401 and then accepts the signed token. The same is true of the approval example. A 401 you see in production for every message usually has one of these causes:

  1. TEAMS_APP_ID is not the bot’s Microsoft Entra application (client) id. The Teams app id in the manifest is a different identifier.
  2. TEAMS_TENANT_ID is not the tenant the bot is registered in. v1 is single-tenant.
  3. The messaging endpoint in the bot registration points at a host that does not forward the Authorization header to the bot.
  4. The host clock is wrong. Token lifetimes are checked against it.

muss-teams doctor checks the first two and the secret’s expiry and tells you which one is wrong without printing a secret. It is read-only.

A 403 from your own bot is often your own code. A bot that registers card handlers but no authorize_card_actions hook answers every action 403, and a bot with no authorize_notifications hook refuses every notification. That is the safe default, not a fault; register the hook. See Card actions and notifications.

If you host the bot yourself, see also your host answers 401.

App not installed

Section titled “App not installed”

Symptoms: AppNotInstalled, NoConversationReference, or a 403 from the Connector or from Graph’s install-scoped endpoints.

  • The SDK never installs the app for you. Installing is a tenant decision and an install over Graph needs an InstallApproval made for that exact request (see directory reads).
  • A proactive message needs a conversation reference. The bot gets one when a person messages it or when it receives the install event. Before that, notify returns NoConversationReference; the proactive example prints before Megan wrote : no conversation reference is stored.
  • After a person blocks the bot, or Teams reports the bot is not in the conversation roster, the stored reference is deleted, and the next call says there is no conversation rather than repeating a 403. Wait for an installationUpdate event. See notifications.
  • muss-teams doctor lists “whether the app is installed anywhere” under not verified. No read it makes can establish that; check in the Teams admin center, and check the tenant’s app permission and setup policies.

Consent missing

Section titled “Consent missing”

Symptoms: MissingPermission, or a 403 from Graph whose message names a permission.

Graph permissions come from the resource, not from the endpoint, and the SDK names the permission its contract needs. Two consent routes exist and they are not interchangeable:

  • Tenant-wide application permissions need an administrator’s consent. The SDK never grants consent. Grant it in the Entra admin center, or ask the administrator, then retry; there is nothing to change in code.
  • Resource-specific consent (RSC) permissions are declared in the app manifest and consented when a team or chat owner installs the app. The manifest must declare them, and RSC permissions that apply to a team do not reach private or shared channels the app was not added to.

permissions and consent lists, per capability, which route applies and which type (delegated or application) it needs. Do not infer one capability’s permissions from another’s; the table is per row.

Two cases look like missing consent and are not:

  • A delegated permission used with an app-only token, or the reverse. The signed-in user’s meeting read, for one, has no application form.
  • A meeting read with no authorize_meeting_reads hook. That is refused locally before any request is sent.

Subscription expiry

Section titled “Subscription expiry”

Symptoms: change notifications stop arriving, with no error.

A Graph subscription is a lease. The service holds it until it expires; the app has to renew it. A Teams message subscription lives for a bounded time (see Microsoft Graph), and a subscription made without a lifecycle notification URL can be renewed only an hour at a time.

  • The SDK starts no timer. You call SubscriptionKeeper::sweep on your own schedule; if the process is down or the sweep is not called, the lease runs out. Call it more often than the renewal window.
  • watch records the subscription so the keeper can renew it. A subscription made through graph.subscriptions() directly is not recorded, so nothing renews it and a restart loses it.
  • The keeper backs off on 401 and transient errors and stops after five failures, telling your recovery hook. A 403 stops it at once, because a person has to grant the permission. A 404 marks the subscription lapsed and it must be created again.
  • Lifecycle items are posted to the lifecycle URL: reauthorizationRequired, subscriptionRemoved and missed. Hand them to SubscriptionKeeper::handle_lifecycle. A missed item means notifications were lost, and the SDK does not claim they can be recovered.
  • The graph_webhook example shows the handshake and a notification reaching the handler against a loopback server.

Card mismatch

Section titled “Card mismatch”

A card can disagree with the client, or with the bot’s own idea of the card.

Symptom Cause What to do
build() fails with a JSON path such as actions[0]: Action.Execute needs version 1.4 but the card declares 1.2 You declared a version lower than a feature needs. Declare the version the card needs, or give the newer element a fallback.
The card builder refuses a card over 100 KB, nested deeper than 32 levels, or declaring a version Teams does not render for a bot The card is outside what the SDK lets you send. Shrink it, or split it.
A press is answered 400 The verb is unknown or missing, and no fallback handler is registered. An unknown verb never reaches another verb’s handler. Register the handler, or on_card_action_fallback.
A press is answered 403 The authorization hook said no, or none is registered. Compare the owner of the object with ctx.actor(), which comes from the signed activity.
A press runs twice Teams retried, or the person pressed twice. Do the work inside ctx.once(..), keyed by the decision.
The data in a press is not what you sent Everything in data except the person’s identity comes from the client and can be changed by anyone. Treat it as untrusted input and check it against your own record.

The SDK builds and checks the JSON; it does not render. It cannot tell you how a card looks on desktop, web or mobile, or what an older client shows. Test the cards you ship on the clients you support. See Adaptive Cards and platform policies.

Ambiguous writes

Section titled “Ambiguous writes”

An AmbiguousWrite means the outcome of a write is not known: the request may have been applied. A timeout, a lost answer or a server error after a send can all produce one.

  • The SDK does not retry it, and is_retryable() is false for it. A blind retry can post the message twice.
  • Reconcile first. Look in the conversation, or look up your own record under the idempotency key you used.
  • notify_with_key makes a request safe to repeat: the same key, target and message returns the first delivery and sends nothing, and an ambiguous attempt is reported as ambiguous again rather than posted again. Use a new key only when you mean to send again.
  • The Connector offers no idempotency key, so the SDK cannot make a send exactly-once. If the process dies after the service accepted the message and before the record says so, a repeat reports ambiguity. For work that must not be lost or doubled, use the outbox pattern in tenant-scoped state.
  • A Retry-After of more than 30 seconds is returned to you, not waited for.

Rich notifications that never reach the handler

Section titled “Rich notifications that never reach the handler”

A rich notification (encrypted resource data) is held unverified until every check passes, then the on_graph_resource handler runs. If nothing arrives, check the GraphFault reports from on_graph_fault: RichRefused carries a fixed reason and never a token, key or payload.

Common causes: no graph_decryption_keys, so watch refuses a request for resource data (ResourceDataNotConfigured); a certificate whose private key you did not load (CertificateNotHeld); a registered thumbprint that does not match the notification (CertificateMismatch); a validation token for another app, tenant or caller; and a signing-key outage, which leaves the entry queued until the keys return. Items older than 24 hours are dropped as QuarantineExpired. See Microsoft Graph.

Dialogs and extensions refuse a request

Section titled “Dialogs and extensions refuse a request”

Dialogs and message extensions arrive as invokes and are answered in the HTTP response. The authorization hook is deny-by-default, as for card actions, so a bot with handlers and no hook answers 403. Beyond that:

  • A submission that was edited by hand is refused; the dialog example shows one.
  • A link whose domain is not on your allowed list is turned away without being fetched; the search_extension example shows one.
  • A person asking for another person’s item is refused; the search example shows that too.
  • A handler past its deadline is cancelled and the person sees “The app took too long to answer.” The limits are in the Bounds section.

See dialogs and extensions.

Meeting and presence refusals

Section titled “Meeting and presence refusals”
  • Meeting reads need the authorize_meeting_reads hook and, for the Connector meeting endpoints, the manifest’s RSC permission for the meeting kind. A 403 from those endpoints means the app is not installed in the meeting, or the call was blocked.
  • A presence read needs Presence.Read.All (or Presence.Read for me()). A presence subscription is delegated, lasts at most 60 minutes, and needs a certificate.
  • Meeting transcripts and recordings are not offered. They depend on a tenant policy the SDK can neither check nor grant.

See meetings.

Your host answers 401

Section titled “Your host answers 401”

When the bot lives inside a server you already run:

  • Forward the Authorization header unchanged to BuiltApp::handle or to the merged router(). A proxy that strips it produces a 401 on every message.
  • Pass the raw body bytes. A body that a framework parsed and re-serialised does not match what the sender signed.
  • Keep the BuiltApp alive. Dropping it aborts its workers; call shutdown(grace) to let in-flight work finish. If a router from router() is still alive, shutdown returns Error::StillInUse.
  • Do not turn authentication off to find the fault. There is no switch for that in a production build, by design.

Setup and packaging

Section titled “Setup and packaging”
  • muss-teams doctor is read-only and prints no secret. It reports a wrong app id, a tenant mismatch, an expired secret, an unset messaging endpoint and a missing service principal where the service exposes enough to tell, and lists what it cannot verify as not verified.
  • muss-teams package stops with an error naming the icon it could not read. It writes to a temporary file and renames it into place, so a failure leaves no partial archive.
  • muss-teams manifest validate names the field that does not match the schema, and an unknown manifest action names itself.

See the command-line tool.
